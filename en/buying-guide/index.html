Who this guide is for

This guide is for buyers from the Netherlands and Belgium who are looking for a home on the western Costa del Sol: from San Pedro de Alcántara, through Estepona, to Sotogrande. Whether you want a second home or a place to spend much of the year, the steps are largely the same. The choices along the way — location, type of property, new or resale — do differ.

Why a trustworthy local agent matters

Buying a home on this coast, especially from another country, depends more than most people expect on the person who shows you properties and talks about price. A weak agent can cost you time and money: asking prices that do not match the market, commissions that only appear later, legal checks that are skipped or rushed, and little real knowledge of the street or the building you are about to buy.

A good local agent does the opposite. They know the area in practice, not only from a portal. They tell you what is wrong as well as what is right. They are open about how they are paid. And they work with your lawyer so the legal checks actually happen before you commit.

Decide first: use and budget

Before you start looking, it helps to settle two things. The first is how you will use the home. An apartment near a promenade can be ideal for longer stays and a life on foot. A villa in an urbanisation, with a car, suits people who want space, quiet or golf. Anyone who is really moving looks at schools, healthcare and everyday shops differently from someone who comes for eight weeks a year.

The second is the budget, including purchase costs. In Andalusia, on a resale home you pay transfer tax (ITP, modalidad TPO) of 7 percent. On a new home you usually pay 10 percent VAT (IVA) plus 1.2 percent stamp duty (AJD): 11.2 percent tax in total. On top come notary fees, land-registry registration and, in most cases, a lawyer’s fee. Those extra items are not a fixed statutory percentage. The often-quoted band of about 10 to 13 percent in extra costs is a direction of travel between those two cases, not a quote. So keep in mind the sum of price and costs, plus a margin — not only the asking price.

Paying with your own funds or with a mortgage also changes the timetable. A Spanish mortgage takes extra time, a valuation and bank conditions. Build that into your plan before you make an offer.

Where to look on this stretch

Westersol works between San Pedro de Alcántara and Sotogrande. That is deliberately narrower than the whole Costa del Sol. Each place has a different pace. The town guides below go further into the areas and recent asking prices.

  • San Pedro de Alcántara — a seaside town, with a long boulevard and, next to it, the residential areas of Guadalmina. Administratively it belongs to Marbella, but it feels like a place in its own right.
  • Estepona — the largest town on this stretch: an old town, a marina, a promenade, and many newer neighbourhoods towards San Pedro and the hills.
  • Sotogrande — more spacious and quieter, in the municipality of San Roque, with a marina, golf and polo. It suits people who want space, less so those who want a Spanish town square as the centre of the day.

Do not go by the town name alone. In Estepona, Idealista put the average asking price in October 2026 at 5,070 euros per square metre, while Altos de Estepona stood at 3,587 euros. An average is not a valuation of one property, but the gap shows that location within the same municipality matters.

Searching and viewing

Start with an honest list: type of property, number of bedrooms, whether you want a garden or a shared pool, how much a walk to the sea or to the shops matters, and how far Málaga airport may be. Keep that list for yourself, and you can also put it on the intake form, so that a local agent is not searching in the dark.

Viewings in person remain essential. Photographs do not catch traffic noise, a slope down to the parking, shade in January, or the condition of the common parts. Visit at different times of day if you can, and ask about the urbanisation: who runs it, what the fees are, and whether there are outstanding works or debts. A lawyer can check that later in writing; you can already ask.

Do not buy in a rush during a holiday week. A development that feels lively in August can feel quiet in January — or the other way around. If you are travelling from further away, leave room between viewings so you can compare without pressure.

The NIE: your foreigner’s identity number

For almost any purchase in Spain you need an NIE: the Número de Identidad de Extranjero, the identity number for foreigners. Without it you cannot, in practice, sign the escritura, pay the taxes, or often put utilities or a bank account cleanly in your name. Apply in good time, not only when the contract is ready.

According to the Spanish Interior Ministry, you can apply for an NIE in Spain, at a police station with authority for foreign nationals, or from abroad at a Spanish consulate. You will need, among other things, the official application form, a valid passport or identity document, and an explanation of why you need the number — for example the purchase of a home. Procedures and appointments vary by office and consulate; check the current instructions on the ministry page or on the site of the consulate that covers you, and involve your lawyer in the timing.

An NIE is a number, not a residence permit. EU citizens may buy a home in Spain without being resident. Whether you later register as a resident is a separate decision, with different tax consequences. That choice belongs with a tax adviser, not with a guide.

A lawyer who acts for you

In Spain a lawyer (abogado) is not a legal requirement when you buy, but for a buyer coming from outside it is wise to instruct one — and someone who acts only for you, not for the seller or the developer. The lawyer checks, among other things, the entry in the land registry, whether mortgages, attachments or debts sit on the property, the planning situation and, for an apartment, the position of the owners’ association.

Agree the fee in advance, in writing. Rules of thumb in percentages circulate; they are not a statutory tariff, and they vary by firm and by file. Do not treat a figure on a website as given. A good lawyer will explain what they will and will not investigate, and when you need a power of attorney (poder) if you cannot be in Spain for every step.

Westersol is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. This guide sets out the steps so that you know what to ask a professional.

Offer, reservation and private contract

Once you have found a home, an offer usually goes through the agent. A spoken agreement does not bind you in the way a signed contract does. When you are serious, there is often a reservation or a private contract (contrato privado, often with an arras arrangement) that sets the price, the timetable, what is included in the sale, and what happens if someone withdraws.

The size of a deposit is not a figure fixed by law. Have your lawyer read every document you sign, and every transfer you make, before you act. Do not pay a deposit into a private account whose role you do not understand. Under many arras contracts the buyer can lose the deposit if they walk away without a valid reason, while the seller may, in some cases, have to repay double. That depends on the clauses; it is not automatic. Have the text translated and explained before you sign, even if you read Spanish well.

Off-plan and new-build purchases come with extra documents: a building permit, a bank guarantee or insurance for sums you pay before completion, and a handover inspection. Ask your lawyer expressly about those protections. Do not buy a plan from a brochure alone.

Taxes and purchase costs

The largest extra item is tax, and it depends on the type of home.

On a resale home (a sale between private parties) the buyer in Andalusia pays transfer tax, the Impuesto sobre Transmisiones Patrimoniales, modalidad TPO. The Junta de Andalucía has applied a general rate of 7 percent since 28 April 2021. That measure, introduced by Ley 5/2021, was still listed as in force in November 2026 on the Junta’s “Medidas vigentes ITPAJD” page.

On a new home, handed over by the business that builds or promotes it, VAT (IVA) usually applies instead of ITP. The Agencia Tributaria, in its 2026 VAT manual, describes the reduced 10 percent rate for the supply of homes (4 percent applies only in specific cases of protected housing). There is also stamp duty, AJD (Actos Jurídicos Documentados), at 1.2 percent in Andalusia since the same 2021 reform. Together that is 11.2 percent tax, still without notary and land registry.

Notary fees follow an official scale that moves with the price; land-registry registration does too. A lawyer charges their own fee. Those items cannot be collapsed into one statutory percentage. That is why we do not write “budget x percent, full stop”. What we can say is this: tax alone is 7 percent on a resale, or 11.2 percent on a typical new home. The extras above come on top. The band of about 10 to 13 percent that you often hear is a direction of travel between those two situations, not a quote for your file.

After completion there are annual costs: municipal property tax (IBI, Impuesto sobre Bienes Inmuebles; each town hall sets its own rate), any community fees for an urbanisation, insurance and utilities. These vary widely. Have them checked before you sign, especially in a complex with a garden or a pool.

Sources, retrieved 25 November 2026: Junta de Andalucía, “Medidas vigentes ITPAJD” and the note on Ley 5/2021; Agencia Tributaria, “Compro una vivienda ¿tengo que pagar IVA o ITP?” and the 2026 VAT manual (reduced 10 percent rate); Ministerio del Interior, “Número de Identidad de Extranjero (NIE)”.

The notary and the escritura

Title passes at the notary, in a public deed: the escritura pública de compraventa. The buyer, the seller (or their attorneys), the notary and usually the lawyer are there. The final payment, the keys and the handover of documents belong to this appointment. The deed is then registered at the land registry (Registro de la Propiedad). That registration is what makes your right visible to third parties; do not treat it as an afterthought.

The notary is a public official. They guarantee the form of the deed, not that you have negotiated the best price or that every building defect has been inspected. A survey, the registry checks and the negotiation remain your responsibility, with the lawyer. If you cannot attend, a power of attorney can be the answer; have it drawn up in good time, with a clear mandate.

How long the whole path takes depends on the NIE, on any financing, on due diligence and on the notary’s diary. Some purchases complete in a few weeks, others take months. Anyone who names a timescale without knowing your file is giving an estimate, not a guarantee.

After completion

After the escritura come the practical steps: transferring water, electricity and, if needed, internet; registering for IBI with the town hall; insurance; and, in an urbanisation, your place in the owners’ association. For a second home it is useful to have someone locally for post, leaks and regular upkeep, especially if you are away for long stretches.

The tax position changes too. As a non-resident you come under Spanish rules on income from real estate, even if you do not let the property — the exact return depends on your situation and on the treaties between Spain and the Netherlands or Belgium. That is work for a tax adviser who knows both countries. Westersol does not give tax advice and would not recommend filing a return from a guide alone.

If you later want to let the property, Andalusia has rules on tourist rentals and, depending on the urbanisation, internal restrictions may apply. Look into that before you buy, not after, if letting is part of your plan.

Common questions

Do I need to be resident in Spain to buy?

No. EU citizens can buy a home in Spain without living there. You do need an NIE, and you must pay the Spanish purchase taxes. Becoming resident is a separate step, with different tax consequences.

Do I need the NIE before I make an offer?

You can view a property without an NIE. For the escritura, the taxes and usually the banking, you do need the number. Apply in good time, in Spain or at a consulate, following the Interior Ministry’s instructions. Do not wait until the deed is booked.

What is the tax difference between a resale and a new home?

On a resale in Andalusia you pay 7 percent transfer tax (ITP-TPO), a rate in force since 28 April 2021. On a typical new home you pay 10 percent VAT plus 1.2 percent AJD, 11.2 percent together, according to the Agencia Tributaria (2026 VAT manual) and the Junta de Andalucía. Notary, land registry and lawyer come on top.

Is “10 to 13 percent in purchase costs” a fixed rule?

No. It is a direction of travel, not a quote. Tax alone is 7 percent or 11.2 percent, depending on resale or new. The other costs vary. Ask your lawyer for an estimate on your file, with the sources beside it.

Can I buy without going to the notary in person?

The escritura takes place at the notary, but you can be represented under a power of attorney. Have your lawyer draw it up, with a clear mandate, and allow extra time for the formalities.
