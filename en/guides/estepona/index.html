Estepona

Estepona is the largest town on the stretch between San Pedro de Alcántara and Sotogrande: an old town, a promenade, a marina and, behind it, the hills of the Sierra Bermeja. It is a place you can live in all year, not only a holiday front.

What is it like?

The old town, with its white streets and flowers, is what gives Estepona its character. Around it lie the promenade, the town beach and the marina. Buyers who want a home to live in — shopping, an evening in the centre, a walk by the sea — often start here. Second-home buyers come too, especially for apartments with light and easy access to the water.

Estepona has also grown quickly in recent years, particularly on the belt towards San Pedro, often called the New Golden Mile, and in neighbourhoods a little inland. That means more choice, but also that you should look closely at location, completion and the condition of an urbanisation. The mountains in the background are a reminder that you can also buy higher and quieter if you want to.

Which areas suit which buyers?

Estepona is large enough to choose badly if you only look at the town name. The zones below are a compass. Idealista published a separate October 2026 figure for Altos de Estepona.

The old town

The casco antiguo suits people who want to live on foot, who are looking for a townhouse or apartment on an Andalusian street, and who value town life more than a large plot. Expect less parking and more character. Due diligence on permits and the condition of the building matters especially here.

The marina, La Rada and the promenade

Closer to the sea and the marina you will mainly find apartments and more recent blocks. This attracts buyers who want the beach, light and a second home, or who want to live in town without the narrow streets of the old core.

Towards San Pedro, and the hills

Between Estepona and San Pedro, and in neighbourhoods a little higher up, there are many more recent complexes and villas. That suits people who want a modern setting and take a car as a given. In Altos de Estepona, inland, Idealista put the average asking price in October 2026 at 3,587 euros per square metre, clearly below Estepona’s urban average of 5,070 euros. Buyers looking for more space for the same budget often look that way — remembering that an average is not a valuation of one property.

Prices

For Estepona as a whole, Idealista recorded an average asking price in October 2026 of 5,070 euros per square metre, 1.0 percent above September and 16.8 percent above October 2025, then the high point in the series. That was the strongest annual rise of the three towns in this guide. Altos de Estepona stood at 3,587 euros per square metre, a good deal lower, and was up only 3.3 percent on the year. The gap shows how much location within the same municipality shapes the asking price.

The buying process and costs for non-residents

The rules below apply across Andalusia, so in San Pedro de Alcántara and Estepona (Málaga province) as well as in Sotogrande (in the municipality of San Roque, Cádiz province). Westersol is a guide, not an estate agency and not a lawyer. A lawyer who acts for you, not for the seller, should confirm the figures on a specific purchase.

  1. The NIE number

    The NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero) is the personal identification number for foreign nationals in Spain. Spain’s Interior Ministry states that you can apply in Spain, in person or through a legal representative, at a foreigners’ office or police station, or from abroad at the Spanish consulate for your place of residence. You need the official form, a valid passport or identity card (original and copy) and a statement of the reason, for example the purchase of a home.

  2. A lawyer who acts for you

    The Spanish notary formalises the deed (escritura) but does not carry out the full due-diligence review you may be used to from a notary in the Netherlands, Belgium or the UK. Have an independent lawyer check title, charges, permits and any unpaid tax or community fees before you commit. Do not use the seller’s lawyer.

  3. Reservation, arras and the escritura

    Once terms are agreed, a reservation or arras contract usually follows, and later the signing of the escritura at the notary. The size of any deposit and what happens if you withdraw are set in that contract; they are not fixed by law as a single percentage. A purchase often takes months, longer if a mortgage or a new-build is involved. After the deed come the taxes and registration at the land registry (Registro de la Propiedad).

Taxes on the purchase

On a resale home in Andalusia you pay transfer tax (ITP, TPO heading). The Junta de Andalucía has, since 28 April 2021 and still in 2026, applied a general rate of 7 percent. On a first delivery of a new home by a developer, VAT (IVA) applies instead. The Spanish tax agency’s 2026 VAT manual sets the general VAT rate on homes at 10 percent (4 percent only for certain officially protected homes). In Andalusia, stamp duty (AJD) is then added, at a general rate of 1.2 percent. ITP or AJD formalities in Andalusia must be filed within two months of the taxable event.

What that means for your budget

Tax alone is 7 percent on a resale, or 10 percent VAT plus 1.2 percent AJD (11.2 percent together) on a new home. On top come notary, land-registry and, usually, legal fees. Those extras are not a fixed statutory percentage: notary fees follow an official scale that varies with the price, and a lawyer’s fee is agreed separately. It is wise to budget a clear margin above the purchase price. On a resale the overall total is often lower than on a new home, because the tax is lower. The widely quoted band of about 10 to 13 percent in extra costs is a direction of travel between those two cases, not a quote.

After completion you should also expect annual municipal property tax (IBI, Impuesto sobre Bienes Inmuebles; each town hall sets its own rate), any community fees for an urbanisation, insurance and utilities. Recurring costs vary widely; have them checked before you sign.

Sources, retrieved 25 November 2026: Junta de Andalucía, “Medidas vigentes ITPAJD”; Agencia Tributaria, “Compro una vivienda ¿tengo que pagar IVA o ITP?” (2026 VAT manual); Ministerio del Interior, “Número de Identidad de Extranjero (NIE)”.
